## Core mechanics

The first hour has 12 Big Walk mechanics and tips worth knowing before you roam.

### Item holding lock

The Item holding lock makes long carries less demanding once you understand its timing. Keep an item overhead for about 10 seconds until an icon fills, then release it to keep the item held. Use the action again when you want to cancel that lock, and treat the feature as help for lengthy item carrying rather than a new objective.

### Slope sliding

Slope sliding begins when you sit on a slope, and it carries you downhill automatically. Remaining stopped keeps the seated state, while using the action again lets you stand. That simple sequence is useful whenever descending quickly is more helpful than walking the same stretch.

### Throw versus kick

Throw versus kick depends on what happens before a charged kick. Holding an item overhead before charging sends it as a throw, while charging directly makes a short-distance kick. Keeping that contrast in mind makes the two supplied outcomes easier to test nearby without assuming any extra rules.

### Cancel kick

## Practical tools

### Lost & Found Pedestal

The Lost & Found Pedestal is in the starting area and receives the item held at the last disconnect. Carry important items before a session ends if you want that supplied behavior to matter for the item you were using. Knowing this location early gives a practical reference point without inventing any additional recovery system.

### Signal flares

Signal flares use an orange terminal plus a red button and launch high with a flash and loud sound. Their stated use is regrouping, so they are a sensible response when people become separated. The useful takeaway is the visible and audible signal, not an assumed distance or extra timing rule.

### Item scanner

The Item scanner is hand-held and identifies interactive nearby items. When you are close, it shows an X-shaped mark, making it especially useful for searching grass. Treat the mark as a nearby clue and keep exploring the immediate space rather than reading more into it than the supplied fact says.

## World mechanics

### Human tower

A Human tower is built by holding teammates, including a teammate who is already holding another teammate. The top player can see farther, and the whole stack can serve as a tall landmark. It is therefore both a cooperative arrangement and a way to make a lost group easier to notice.

### Day-night cycle

The Day-night cycle gives day a duration of about 8 minutes and dusk a duration of about 6 minutes. Sunset is worth pausing to watch, which is the only recommendation attached to those supplied timings. There is no need to rush that moment or turn the schedule into a stricter route than the guide supports.

### No fall damage

No fall damage means falling does not remove health, though it does drop held items. Small objects can vanish in grass, so remember the place where you fell if something was lost during the drop. This is a reminder to track the fall location, not a promise that every item will remain easy to spot.

The practical difference is between health and the object currently being carried. A fall leaves health unchanged, yet the carried object can leave your possession and small objects may disappear into grass. Remembering the fall location keeps the response tied to the supplied fact: return to the place where the object was dropped. It does not claim a recovery guarantee, identify an item location, or add a rule beyond the dropped-item behavior.

## Playing together

### Player-count balancing

Player-count balancing changes puzzle difficulty according to the number of people in the session. A supplied three-person puzzle can use two people in a two-player session, but changing the count requires restarting the session. Share that constraint with the group before reorganizing, since it matters more than assuming every puzzle keeps one fixed arrangement.

This balancing note is deliberately narrow. It says the puzzle difficulty responds to the session count and gives the supplied two-person use of a three-person puzzle as its example. It also says a count change requires a restart, which is the point to discuss before a group reorganizes. Nothing here assigns a solution, a location, or a different puzzle arrangement beyond that stated example.

### Red stairs

The red stairs mark the way out of the starting area, with the required object at their top. A mechanism locks that object until four red objects from four area puzzles are used. This gives the first red-area puzzle a clear purpose while keeping the guide within the stated starting-area goal.

## First-hour advice

Finish the tutorial, then use the immediate surroundings for nearby tests before moving farther away. Find Lost & Found early so the group recognizes the starting-area reference point and understands why important items should be carried before a session ends. These first checks make the supplied mechanics familiar without requiring a fixed learning order. Explore without rushing, discuss what each person notices, and keep friends informed as the group changes direction.

Begin the first red-area puzzle when the group is ready, after the tutorial and nearby testing have established a comfortable start. Communication matters most when people separate, because it makes a regrouping choice clear instead of leaving anyone to guess where the group went. Use a flare when its high, bright, loud signal will help people notice one another, or make a human tower when a tall landmark and a farther view are more useful. Both options support friends who are already trying to reconnect.

There is no one wrong way to play the first hour, so the tutorial, Lost & Found, nearby testing, and the first red-area puzzle are a flexible rhythm rather than a race. A group can pause its exploration, communicate, then return to the next immediate goal without losing the purpose of the guide. Keeping the pace calm leaves space to test the surrounding mechanics and to use a flare or human tower only when someone is lost. The useful result is shared orientation, not a rigid route.
