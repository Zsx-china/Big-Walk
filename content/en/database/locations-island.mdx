Big Walk's island is one continuous open world, styled and scaled after
Australia's **Wilsons Promontory National Park** — the southernmost tip of
the Australian mainland. There are no loading screens between areas: the
route is physical, and the landmarks stay put.

![Big Walk island treehouse bridge in the forest](/images/big-walk-treehouse-bridge.jpg)

## The landmark set

| Landmark | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| **Camp** | Your starting anchor and mental home base |
| **Red drawbridge** | The first hard gate, keyed by four figures |
| **Blue doorway / Map Room** | Locked until the red tower is done |
| **Red tower** | First tower, five red collectibles |
| **Green tower** | Second tower, easier commitment |
| **Yellow tower** | Hardest tower |
| **The summit** | The route's end (details pending) |

## How the world teaches you its layout

- **Sight lines matter.** Towers are visible from each other, and the game
  uses line-of-sight the way a national park uses peaks — see it, walk to it.
- **Audio marks routes.** Water usually marks a route, echoes mark enclosed
  spaces, and gear sounds mark mechanisms.
- **Colour is a language.** Red, green, and yellow objects exist in the
  world *before* their towers do — the island is a walking museum of its own
  puzzle logic.

<Callout type="info" label="FIELD NOTE">
Because the world is open and unsegmented, "where are we?" is a real
question — which is exactly why the Map Room unlock feels like a milestone
rather than a convenience.
</Callout>

## Getting around

Navigation is by landmark, not minimap. The practical toolkit:

1. Keep the towers as your compass (they're visible from most of the map).
2. Use the Map Room overview when planning a leg.
3. Mark camp as home and return to it as a group between objectives.

The island is also worth walking just to walk — that's rather the point.
