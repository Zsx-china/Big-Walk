Let's get the short answer out of the way: **Big Walk has no classes.** There
are no character types, no skill trees, no stats, and no loadouts. Every
walker carries everything a walker can carry, and everyone can do every
puzzle.

What *does* exist is role-play — not in the menu, but in how groups
organize themselves on the trail. The best parties naturally fall into a
handful of roles, and naming them makes coordination easier.

## The emergent roles

| Role | What they do | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Scout** | Runs ahead, marks landmarks, reports routes back | Larger groups (7–12) |
| **Navigator** | Owns the Map Room and the route plan | Groups after the red tower |
| **Caller** | Directs puzzles from a fixed viewpoint | Any puzzle with a chasm |
| **Carrier** | Moves figures and collectibles between slots | Groups with long hauls |
| **Anchor** | Stays at the meeting point and coordinates by walkie-talkie | Groups that split up |

## How to assign roles without drama

- **Let people volunteer.** The Scout role attracts the runners; the Anchor
  role attracts the patient. Both are needed.
- **Rotate the Caller** every couple of puzzles — calling is tiring, and
  rotating keeps everyone involved.
- **One role per person per segment.** A player who is both Carrier and
  Caller is doing two jobs and will drop the key.

<Callout type="tip" label="GROUP SIZE RULE">
At 2–3 players, skip formal roles entirely — the walk itself is the
coordination. Roles start paying off at 5+, where someone will be out of
proximity range of the action.
</Callout>

## "Classes" in the wiki's sense

If you came here from a search for "Big Walk classes", the useful frame is:
the class system is *communication-based*. The closest thing to a "tank"
is the player who carries the key; the closest thing to a "healer" is the
player who repeats directions until everyone is aligned. Build your party
composition around that, and you'll never need a stat sheet.
